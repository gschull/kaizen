# 🔧 Challenges & Solutions

Common issues you’ll hit when practicing kaiZEN (solo or with a group), plus simple fixes.

---

## Getting started

### “This feels overwhelming”

**Fix:** Reduce the step until it feels almost silly.

- Make it a 30–60 second action
- Track “did it happen?” not “was it perfect?”
- If you fail twice, shrink the step

---

### “I don’t know what to improve”

**Fix:** Start with friction.

- Pick one daily annoyance
- Ask: “What’s the smallest change that would reduce this?”
- Try it for 7 days, then adjust

---

## Consistency

### “I keep forgetting”

**Fix:** Attach kaiZEN to an existing cue.

- After coffee → log a habit
- After lunch → 1-minute tidy
- Before bed → write one reflection line

---

### “I missed a day and gave up”

**Fix:** Use “never miss twice.”

- Missing once is normal
- Your only rule: do the next one

---

## Groups

### “People aren’t participating equally”

**Fix:** Keep group rituals small and opt-in.

- One weekly review topic
- Celebrate wins publicly
- Avoid blame; talk about systems

---

## Quick Reference: Solution Strategies

| Problem Type | Primary Strategy |
|--------------|------------------|
| Resistance to starting | Lead by example, don't push |
| Forgetting | Visual reminders, habit linking |
| Losing interest | Simplify, add fun |
| Kid won't participate | Find their motivation, give autonomy |
| Partner resistant | Start without them, share results |
| Too overwhelming | Reduce, reduce, reduce |
| Doesn't stick | Make it smaller, change environment |
| Lost momentum | Restart without guilt, celebrate progress |

---

## The Ultimate Solution

When all else fails, remember the core of Kaizen:

> **Make it smaller.**

Whatever you're trying to do, make it smaller. Then smaller again. Until it's impossible to fail.

- Can't do a 15-minute meeting? Do 5 minutes.
- Can't do 5 minutes? Do 2 minutes.
- Can't do 2 minutes? Ask one question at dinner.
- Can't do that? Just pause and take one breath together.

The size of the step matters far less than taking the step.
